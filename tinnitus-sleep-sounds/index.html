Tinnitus sleep sounds

Tinnitus is usually at its worst when everything else is quiet, which is exactly when you are trying to sleep. The room is silent, there is nothing else at your frequency to attend to, and the ringing becomes the loudest thing in the house.

Which sounds actually help

The useful property is steadiness. A sound with no events in it — no words, no melody, nothing that resolves — gives your hearing something to settle on without giving your attention anything to follow.

  • Brown noise — deep and soft, the least fatiguing over hours. A common favourite for high-pitched tinnitus.
  • Pink noise — between brown and white, closer to rainfall.
  • White noise — the brightest, and the most likely to feel harsh at 3 a.m.
  • Rain, fan, ocean, wind — the same idea with more texture, which some people find easier to ignore.

Podcasts and music work for falling asleep and then work against you: speech and melody keep pulling attention back, and both have gaps your tinnitus fills.

How loud?

Quieter than you think. Aim for a level that sits just under your tinnitus — enough that the ringing stops being the only thing in the room, not enough to bury it. Sound loud enough to cover tinnitus completely is usually too loud to run for eight hours, and your ears will tell you so in the morning.

Setting a phone up for the night

  • Decide what happens at the end. A timer that fades out is kinder than one that stops dead — a sudden silence can wake you as effectively as a noise.
  • All night, or fade? If you wake at 3 a.m., all-night playback means the sound is already there. If you sleep straight through, a fade saves your battery.
  • Check it survives the screen locking, and that it does not stop when a notification arrives.
  • Generated beats looped. A ten-minute audio file loops, and once you have noticed the seam you will keep noticing it. Sound synthesized as it plays has no seam.
  • Mind the battery. Overnight playback plus a phone that aggressively kills background apps is a bad combination; exempt the app from battery optimisation if it keeps stopping.
